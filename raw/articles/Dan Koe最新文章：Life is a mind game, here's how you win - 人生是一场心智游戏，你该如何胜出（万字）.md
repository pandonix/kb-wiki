Why most people won't make it

为什么大多数人无法成功

By DAN KOE Apr 26, 2026

全文翻译

At some point, usually in your 20s, you’ll notice that the people around you stop believing in themselves. And no matter how hard you try, you can’t save them. By all means, do not let it infect your mind. Stay on your path.

通常在二十多岁的某个时刻，你会发现身边的人不再相信自己。而无论你多努力，都无法拯救他们。无论如何，别让这种心态影响你的心态，坚持走自己的路。

I was mulling over that thought a few days ago. And the more I think about it, the deeper the problem goes. I can’t help but notice that too many people between the ages of 20 and 35 fall into the blatantly obvious vicious cycle.

前几天我一直在琢磨这个想法，越想越觉得问题根深蒂固。我不禁发现，太多 20 到 35 岁的人，陷入了显而易见的恶性循环。

You get motivated by some external source. A video, a book, a conversation, whatever. You feel like this is your moment. You go all in. You start the business. You start working out. You start learning the skill.

你会被某个外部因素激发动力：一段视频、一本书、一次对话，诸如此类。你觉得这是属于你的时刻，于是全力以赴 —— 创业、健身、学技能。

But then like clockwork, 2 days, a week, 2 weeks later... you fall off. You so easily go back to the life you swore you despised.

但就像精准的钟表，两天、一周、两周后…… 你就半途而废，轻易回到那个你曾发誓厌恶的生活里。

As if you were addicted to it. As if you preferred it over the life you just said you wanted. As if the desire to change your life was just another form of cheap dopamine that you couldn’t resist.

仿佛你对旧生活上瘾，仿佛你更偏爱它，胜过你刚说想要的新生活。仿佛改变人生的渴望，只是另一种你无法抗拒的廉价多巴胺。

Life goes on. You get another hit of motivation. You start again, from zero. The cycle repeats. You get frustrated, but not enough to solidify the change.

日子继续过，你又被打了一剂 “动力针”，从零开始重新出发。循环不断重复，你感到沮丧，却没沮丧到足以彻底做出改变。

Then you wake up. One, two, ten years later. What’s going on? Why is this such a common thing? Why is it so damn hard to change your life?

然后你猛然惊醒，一年、两年、十年过去了。到底怎么回事？为什么这如此普遍？为什么改变人生就这么难？

I’ll tell you why, and it’s not anything you’ve heard before. Another video about productivity, motivation, discipline, or dopamine doesn’t even solve half the equation. You’ve watched enough of those, and nothing’s changed.

我来告诉你原因，这是你从未听过的答案。再多讲效率、动力、自律或多巴胺的视频，连问题的一半都解决不了。那些东西你已经看得够多了，但生活却毫无改变。

We need to dig much, much deeper into the mind.

我们必须往心智的更深处挖掘。

By the end of this letter, I hope you will have what you need to adopt the traits of a wildly successful person. Specifically, a person with this admirable quality:

读完这封信，我希望你能具备成为顶尖成功者的特质，尤其是拥有这种令人敬佩的品质：

To work with tremendous intensity on things that matter to you, and more importantly, to be strangely unbothered when those things don’t work out.

对真正重要的事全力以赴，更重要的是，当事情不如愿时，能异常淡然。

If you actually attempt to understand this letter rather than just read it to horde knowledge, your life will look drastically different in 3-6 months.如果你真正试着理解这封信，而不只是为了囤积知识而阅读，3 到 6 个月内，你的人生会截然不同。

Your mind is a collection of survival strategies, that’s why it’s so hard to change.

你的心智是一套生存策略的集合，这就是改变如此艰难的原因。

Survival is the mechanical, often unconscious, engine behind every thought, emotion, and action. Everything you think, feel, or do is rooted in survival.

生存是驱动你每一个想法、情绪和行为的机械且往往无意识的引擎。你所有的所思、所感、所为，都根植于生存本能。

I repeat, your entire human experience, right now, is directed by survival. This is one of the most important topics you can understand to change your life, because it’s the root of what’s keeping you the same.

我再说一遍：你此刻全部的人生体验，都由生存本能主导。这是你改变人生必须理解的核心课题，因为它是你停滞不前的根源。

There are two types of survival. People tend to only think of the first one.生存分为两种，人们通常只想到第一种。

1. Physical survival – Like animals, we attempt to reproduce the information in our genes. This is largely solved in today’s world. Most reading this aren’t worried about getting eaten or dying from starvation.

1. 生理生存—— 像动物一样，我们试图复制基因里的信息。在当今世界，这一点基本已解决。读这篇文章的大多数人，不用担心被吃掉或饿死。

2. Psychological/conceptual survival – We also attempt to reproduce the information in our consciousness. We feel the drive to preserve our non-physical identity.

2. 心理/ 概念生存 —— 我们也试图复制意识里的信息，本能地想要守护非物质层面的自我身份。

The ideas, beliefs, practices, and worldview that shape our mind.To understand this, we need to understand the gist of evolution.

观念、信念、行为模式和世界观塑造了我们的心智。要理解这一点，我们需要明白进化的本质。

Billions of years ago, some self-replicating molecules existed. Some replicated more successfully than others. The ones that happened to build protective structures around themselves (cell walls, then cells, then multicellular bodies) replicated more than the ones that didn’t.

数十亿年前，地球上出现了一些可自我复制的分子，其中一部分复制得更成功。那些恰好为自己构建了保护结构（细胞壁、细胞、多细胞躯体）的分子，比没有保护结构的复制得更多。

Over billions of iterations, you end up with a world full of genes that are really good at building physical bodies that are able to survive.

经过数十亿次迭代，世界上充满了擅长构建可生存躯体的基因。

Then, human consciousness became a thing. Don’t ask me how. I don’t know.

后来，人类意识出现了。别问我怎么出现的，我也不知道。

But the same process continued. We have genes that selected and self-replicated. Then we have memes. You can think of them as genes for the mind.

但同样的过程仍在继续：我们有不断筛选与自我复制的基因，也有 “模因”—— 你可以把它理解为心智的基因。

I’m not talking about memes that you find on Instagram and send to your friend. I’m talking about the original meaning, coined by Richard Dawkins, which is defined as “units of culture that spread from mind to mind.”

我说的不是社交平台上转发的梗图，而是理查德・道金斯提出的原始定义：在心智之间传播的文化单元。

Memes are the fabric of the social world. Memes are the ideas, words, language, concepts, beliefs, and worldviews that allow you to make sense of and survive in the world.

模因是社会的构成基石，是让你理解世界、在世界中生存的想法、词汇、语言、概念、信念与世界观。

When you were a child, your parents spread their memes to you. Their beliefs, their ideas of what success means, their ideas of what failure means, and the language they speak that allowed you to communicate within your culture.

小时候，父母把他们的模因传递给你：他们的信念、对成功与失败的定义，以及让你在文化中沟通的语言。

Most of these were installed in your head before you even had the time to question them. You just think “this is how life is” when it’s really been a process of natural selection that has led to how we speak, relate, and communicate today.

这些东西大多在你有能力质疑前，就已经植入脑海。你以为 “人生本该如此”，但其实这是自然选择的结果，塑造了我们如今的表达、相处与沟通方式。

Then you went to school. You were rewarded for good performance, solidifying certain memes that benefited the survival goals of society, and punished for bad performance so you didn’t pose a threat.

后来你上学，表现好会被奖励，固化了符合社会生存目标的模因；表现差会被惩罚，避免你成为威胁。

The most dangerous set of memes right now is the classic go to school, get a job, retire at 65 which represent the pinnacle of success.

当下最危险的一套模因，就是经典的 “上学→工作→65 岁退休”，并把这当作成功的顶峰。

Anything less is considered failure by those who have not evolved beyond the industrial set of memes.

没能做到这一点，在那些没跳出工业时代模因的人眼里，就是失败。

In today’s world of rapid advancement and AI, those are no longer beneficial to success and survival. Culture is evolving.

在如今快速发展与 AI 普及的时代，这套模式不再利于成功与生存，文化正在进化。

One important survival mechanism of memes are institutions and ideologies.

模因的重要生存机制之一，是制度与意识形态。

We create larger structures like religions, nations, political parties, and corporations that have the goal of protecting, perpetuating, and replicating the beliefs and values they were founded on.

我们构建宗教、国家、政党、企业等更大的体系，目的是守护、延续、复制其创立时的信念与价值观。

They try to spread their memes to you, and if they are successful, you’re more likely to attempt to spread them to others, because they are now a part of who you are.

它们试图把模因传递给你，一旦成功，你就会主动传播给别人，因为这些模因已经成为你的一部分。

They are something you’ve identified with and hold as a part of your “self”

(a composition of memes that create a psychological structure/body ).

你认同它们，把它们当作 “自我” 的一部分 —— 一套构建心理结构 / 心智躯体的模因组合。

Religions want more members, nations want more control, corporations want more customers.

宗教想要更多信徒，国家想要更多掌控，企业想要更多客户。

A religion that tells its adherents to have many children and convert outsiders will spread faster than a religion that tells its adherents to remain celibate and keep quiet, obviously.

显然，要求信徒多生育、向外传教的宗教，会比要求信徒独身、沉默的宗教传播得更快。

Same with political ideologies, brands, conspiracy theories, jokes, fashions, video games, coffee, alcohol, team Apple, team Android, team Claude, team ChatGPT, team anti-AI, keto, carnivore, vegan, your morning routine, and really anything else.

政治理念、品牌、阴谋论、笑话、时尚、游戏、咖啡、酒精、苹果党、安卓党、Claude 派、ChatGPT 派、反 AI 派、生酮饮食、食肉饮食、素食主义、你的晨间习惯…… 几乎所有事物都是如此。

Understanding this is largely a key to get what you want in life be it money, fame, or success.

理解这一点，是获得人生所求（金钱、名声、成功）的关键。

Everything is a religion. Your morning routine is a religion. Your political opinions are a religion. Your identity as a gamer, a lifter, a minimalist, a stoic, a craft beer enthusiast... all religions.

一切都是 “信仰”：你的晨间习惯是信仰，政治观点是信仰，你作为玩家、健身者、极简主义者、斯多葛主义者、精酿爱好者的身份…… 全都是信仰。

With social media and global access to information, we’re experiencing the religionization of everything.

随着社交媒体与全球信息互通，我们正经历万物信仰化。

Very few people actually do things because they want to anymore. Almost everyone’s life is built around actions that allows them to fit into the digital tribe.

如今很少有人真的因为想做而去做事，几乎所有人的生活，都围绕着融入网络圈层的行为而构建。

The point: Genes have gotten really good at survival. Memes have gotten really really good at survival. They’re primary goal is to persist.

核心是：基因极擅长生存，模因极其擅长生存，它们的首要目标就是存续。

I think you get it. How does this relate to the vicious cycle of trying to achieve your goals and quickly going back to your old ways?

我想你懂了。这和 “努力追求目标又快速退回旧习惯” 的恶性循环有什么关系？

Because the mind is a self-deception machine.

因为心智是一台自我欺骗的机器。

When you try to pursue a goal that would fundamentally change who you are, it’s very difficult to notice, because your mind doesn’t want you to notice, but your ego starts throwing out defensive reactions left and right.

当你追求一个会彻底改变自我的目标时，你很难察觉 —— 因为心智不想让你察觉，但你的自我会开始四处发起防御。

Your mind is threatened. Your identity is threatened. Your memes want to survive, and their goal is to persist.

你的心智感到威胁，身份感到威胁，你的模因想要生存，目标就是存续。

Your mind floods with anxiety, fear of failure, irrational thinking, and the closest distraction that will give you a hit of quick comfort. Like an addict going through withdrawals.

你的心智被焦虑、对失败的恐惧、非理性想法淹没，还会抓住最易得的消遣，换取短暂安慰，就像瘾君子戒断时的状态。

When you are physically attacked, your body goes into fight or flight. You do anything and everything you can to survive.

当身体受到攻击，你会进入战斗或逃跑模式，拼尽全力求生。

When you are psychologically attacked, the same thing happens. Your mind reacts as if you were just shoved, punched, shot, or stabbed depending on the severity of the threat to your ego/identity.

当心理受到攻击时，反应一模一样：根据自我受到威胁的程度，心智会像被推搡、殴打、枪击、刺伤一样应激。

If you were to deeply observe your reactions, you would realize that they are all dictated by survival. Every single thought you have or action you take centers around the survival of your self-image.

如果你深入观察自己的反应，会发现全由生存本能支配：你每一个想法、每一个行为，都围绕着自我形象的生存。

Now, imagine if you were able to reprogram this process so that the opposite happened.

现在想象一下：如果你能重新编程这个过程，让结果反过来。

What if you felt threatened when you stopped pursuing your meaningful goal? So much so that achieving the goal didn’t require much discipline, because it’s just a part of who you are?

如果你停止追求有意义的目标时，反而感到威胁？威胁大到让你不需要太多自律就能达成目标，因为这已经成为你的一部分？

You can’t escape the mind game, but you can master it

你无法逃离这场心智游戏，但你可以掌控它

Effortless self discipline happens when the desire to become the highest version of yourself outweighs the desires of the lowest version of yourself.

当成为最好版本的自己的渴望，胜过成为最差版本的渴望时，毫不费力的自律就会出现。

The core difference between “winners” and “losers” is that losers are afraid of success while winners are afraid of mediocrity.

“赢家” 和 “输家” 的核心区别：输家害怕成功，赢家害怕平庸。

The person who does not identify as a fit person will feel threatened when they make the commitment to be healthy.

不认为自己是健康人士的人，一旦承诺要健康生活，就会感到威胁。

You may not consciously identify as an “unfit person,” but you identify with the preferences, habits, and abilities (the properties of the self) that an unfit person would have.

你可能不会刻意觉得自己 “不健康”，但你认同不健康的人所拥有的偏好、习惯与能力（自我属性）。

So your mind starts sending warning signals. What happens to going out and partying with friends? What happens to the late-night snacks while watching Netflix?

于是心智开始发出警告：和朋友外出派对怎么办？边看网飞边吃夜宵怎么办？

What happens to relaxing on the couch instead of going for a walk? Even the little things... what happens to adding a slab of butter to your favorite meal? I really have to give up that extra flavor?

窝在沙发休息而不去散步怎么办？就连小事 —— 最爱吃的饭不能加一大块黄油怎么办？我真的要放弃这份美味吗？

And when the opportunity does come to go out, indulge at night, or relax on the couch, are you going to be able to break that deeply ingrained pattern?

当外出、深夜放纵、窝沙发的机会真的来临时，你能打破这种根深蒂固的模式吗？

These are four out of thousands of thoughts that would enter the unfit person’s mind.

这只是不健康的人脑海中万千念头里的四个。

However, the fit person would feel threatened by the opposite. This baffles the unfit person. They don’t understand it.

但健康的人，会因为不这么做而感到威胁，这让不健康的人困惑，无法理解。

If the fit person travels to a place that doesn’t have readily available clean food and a gym, they feel like all of their gains are going to disappear.

健康的人去到没有干净食物和健身房的地方，会觉得自己的所有成果都要消失。

They feel like if they eat McDonalds once, they’ll spontaneously combust.他们会觉得吃一次麦当劳，就会前功尽弃。

This is largely the secret of successful individuals.

这在很大程度上，就是成功者的秘诀。

The businessman would feel like a failure if their business started losing revenue, so much so that they would obsess over tactics, strategies, and plugging holes in their business until revenue started to increase.

商人如果生意开始亏损，会觉得自己失败，执念于方法、策略、填补漏洞，直到营收回升。

They wouldn’t be able to sleep at night until that problem is solved.

问题不解决，晚上就睡不着。

How did you get to this point? We’ve discussed it briefly, but when you were young, people expected you to act, think, and behave a certain way.你是怎么变成这样的？我们简单说过：小时候，身边人期待你按特定方式思考、行动、做事。

Your parents were influenced by their parents and culture. Your parents' worldview was passed on to you as a child.

父母受他们的父母和文化影响，又把世界观传递给小时候的你。

You started growing up, maybe you rebelled, but you couldn’t escape the subtle programming from your teachers, peers, and, even worse in today’s world, social media.

长大后你或许叛逆过，却逃不开老师、同辈的潜移默化，在如今的时代，更逃不开社交媒体的塑造。

You could effectively search up any information on the internet and program yourself into being a part of a tribe whose general actions aren’t beneficial to a meaningful and useful future.

你能在网上搜到任何信息，把自己编程成某个圈层的一员，而这个圈层的行为，往往不利于你拥有有意义、有价值的未来。

The good thing is, if we want to flip the script from being the person who is threatened by “success goal" to becoming the person who is threatened by a “failure goal", we can consciously use this process to reprogram our own minds.

好消息是：如果我们想反转剧本 —— 从被 “成功” 目标威胁的人，变成被 “失败” 目标威胁的人，我们可以有意识地用这个过程重塑心智。

I want to teach you how to weaponize your survival mechanism. That way, you can intentionally identify with things that trigger threat responses that push you in a positive direction. You’ll get results, but you’ll still suffer, just more impressively.

我想教你如何把生存机制变成武器，主动认同那些能触发正向威胁反应的事物，推动自己前进。你会取得成果，但仍会痛苦，只是更有意义。

The third section is about what to do about that. Don’t skip to it. You can’t transcend a game you haven’t learned to play.

第三部分会讲如何应对，别跳过 —— 你无法超越一场还没学会玩的游戏。

With that said, this won’t happen overnight. You will have withdrawals. You will have relapses. But these are what I’ve found the most potent for recreating yourself.

话虽如此，改变不会一夜发生，你会经历戒断反应，会反复，但这些方法是我发现重塑自我最有效的方式。

All of these overlap in some way and synergize with each other.

这些方法彼此重叠、相互协同。

1 – You need a reason with extremely high gravitational pull, and when that one fades, you must find another.

1 - 你需要一个极具吸引力的理由，当它消失时，必须找到下一个

The most intense periods of my life came after I stumbled across a reason to change. A reason with such an intense pull that I couldn’t focus on anything else but actualizing the positive trajectory of my life.

我人生中最专注的阶段，都是偶然找到改变的理由之后。这个理由吸引力极强，让我只能专注于实现人生的正向轨迹。

The thing is, this reason doesn’t last forever. You can quickly find yourself back in a rut months later because you let autopilot take over.

问题是，这个理由不会永远持续，几个月后你可能又陷入平庸，因为你让惯性接管了人生。

It’s difficult to force this reason. You have to find it in a book, conversation, or experience that happens at such a perfect moment that everything clicks for you.

这个理由很难刻意强求，你要在某本书、某次对话、某次经历的恰好时刻找到它，让你豁然开朗。

You have to discover it by breaking out of the familiar past and predictable future.

你必须跳出熟悉的过去和可预见的未来，才能找到它。

For most people, right when they wake up, they are met with a thought that triggers a behavior, and they start the day as their old self.

对大多数人来说，一醒来就被一个念头触发行为，以旧的自我开始一天。

You think about the commute to work, which was stressful for you in the past, so you bring that feeling into the present, and now you are stressed and narrow-minded.

你想到上班通勤，过去的压力涌上心头，把压力带到当下，变得焦虑、狭隘。

Then the cascade continues. You experience the feelings and behaviors of your past, which create a predictable future that you don’t want.

连锁反应持续，你重复过去的感受与行为，创造出你不想要的可预见未来。

Only in the unknown will you find a novel insight that will launch you into the next phase of your life.

只有在未知中，你才能找到全新的洞见，推动你进入人生下一阶段。

How do you start to live in the unknown?

如何开始活在未知里？

2 – You need to become brutally aware of who you don’t want to become.

2 - 你必须清醒地知道，自己不想成为什么样的人

The most powerful visualization exercise you can do is this: For the next day, look at all of your current actions. Look at them all.What you do when you wake up, what you do throughout the day, what you do at night.

你能做的最有力的可视化练习是：接下来一整天，观察你所有的行为，全部观察。醒来做什么、白天做什么、晚上做什么。

Now, sit with a pen and paper and write down exactly where your life is going to end up if you keep doing the same thing.

然后拿纸笔坐下，写下：如果继续这样生活，你的人生最终会变成什么样。

If you’re honest with yourself, the answer will probably disgust you. And that disgust can be used as an ultrapotent fuel to slingshot in a positive direction for quite a long time, until you need a new reason.

如果你对自己诚实，答案可能会让你厌恶。而这份厌恶，能成为极强的动力，推动你长期正向前进，直到你需要新的理由。

The problem here is that most people are dishonest. It doesn’t really hit them. Why? Because the self doing the exercise is the self being evaluated.

问题是大多数人对自己不诚实，无法真正触动。为什么？因为做练习的自我，正是被评估的自我。

You’re asking the current identity to honestly assess where the current identity is heading, but the current identity has a survival interest in not seeing this clearly, because seeing it clearly would mean death.

你让当下的自我诚实地评估自己的走向，但当下的自我出于生存本能，不想看清，因为看清意味着 “旧自我的死亡”。

You soften the blow. You rationalize it. You imagine future variables that may rescue you, so changing right now doesn’t really matter.

你会弱化打击、自我合理化、幻想未来的变数拯救自己，觉得现在不改变也没关系。

For this exercise to work, you have to be able to receive the honest answer. You have to observe people in your real life so that you can actually see what your future looks like.

这个练习要起效，你必须接受真实答案，观察现实中的人，看清自己的未来模样。

And, you need something that has already created a crack. So when you do this exercise, it splits you right open.

而且你需要一个已经出现的 “裂痕”，让这个练习彻底击穿你。

What creates the crack? For most people, something catastrophic and real has to happen before they change.

裂痕从何而来？对大多数人来说，只有真实的重大变故发生，才会改变。

Their car has to break down, or they have a health scare, or a parent dies, or a relationship ends that makes you want to get super jacked. That’s what creates the crack. You can’t be living mindlessly.

车坏了、健康警报、亲人离世、关系破裂…… 才会让你想要彻底改变。这就是裂痕，你不能浑浑噩噩地活着。

Let’s solve that.

我们来解决这个问题。

3 – You need to change your environment faster than the identity can recalibrate.

3 - 你必须在身份重新适应前，快速改变环境

Your old self is partly stored in your surroundings. The people you follow online. Your bed. Your routine. The people you hang out with.

旧的自我一部分储存在环境里：你关注的人、你的床、你的习惯、你的社交圈。

That’s a big one and points to why so many people aren’t where they want to be in life. Your identity is often programmed socially.

这是关键，也是很多人无法活成理想模样的原因：你的身份往往由社交环境塑造。

The same person in two different friend groups will behave like two different people. Moreso, when you log on to social media, you also become a different person.

同一个人在不同朋友圈里，会像两个人；在社交媒体上，又会变成另一个人。

The simplest move here is to wipe your phone or get a new one. And once you do, only download what’s necessary.

最简单的做法：清空手机或换一部新手机，只下载必要的东西。

Don’t go back to the same social media platforms, accounts, and websites you used to.

别再用以前的社交平台、账号、网站。

The most difficult move that many successful individuals make is to spend a week somewhere new, even if it’s only a few hours away from you in an Airbnb.

很多成功者做的最难的事：去新地方待一周，哪怕只是离家几小时车程的民宿。

You just have to break the cycle of repeating the same day over and over again by removing the triggers that keep that cycle going.

你必须移除维持循环的触发点，打破日复一日重复的模式。

Beyond that: If you want to change your life fast, the most useful thing you can do is immerse your mind in the environment of your future self.

除此之外：想快速改变人生，最有效的方法是让心智沉浸在未来自我的环境里。

Bathe your psyche in the opinions, beliefs, and education that person would have without judgement.

让心灵浸泡在未来自我会拥有的观点、信念、学识里，不做评判。

At first, I was curious about starting a business. I searched for courses and took a few, but it wasn’t until everything I read, every video I watched on a walk, everyone I followed on social media, and all of the conversations I had were about business.

起初我只是好奇创业，搜了些课程学，但直到我读的、散步看的视频、关注的人、聊的话题全都是商业时，改变才真正发生。

I would pick up tips and tricks, but more importantly, I felt like if I wasn’t building a business, I didn’t fit into the environment I was in.

我学到了技巧，但更重要的是：我觉得不创业，就融入不了自己所处的环境。

Identity is created through learning. You can’t learn if you react all the time or think you know everything.

身份通过学习塑造，如果你总情绪化反驳或自以为无所不知，就无法学习。

You must willingly expose yourself to perspectives you disagree with, and try to understand them.

你必须主动接触不认同的观点，并试着理解。

4 – You need to increase the gap between impulse and response.

4 - 你必须拉长冲动与回应之间的距离

Break the pattern today, or you will repeat the loop tomorrow.

今天打破模式，否则明天继续循环。

The way you start detaching from your old patterns is to do nothing. Correct, nothing.

脱离旧模式的起点，就是什么都不做—— 没错，什么都不做。

This is the only way you can interrupt the survival mechanism in real time.

这是唯一能实时中断生存机制的方法。

Everything else, from finding a reason to changing your environment, happens outside the moment of the urge to remain your past self.

其他方法（找理由、换环境）都发生在 “想维持旧自我” 的冲动之外，能创造更好的条件。

They create better conditions, but they don’t help you in the 3 seconds when your hand is reaching for your phone.

但在你伸手拿手机的那 3 秒里，它们帮不上忙。

Your job is to starve the old self that is trying to survive of the normal feedback it receives.

你的任务是：不给试图生存的旧自我提供惯常反馈。

This is a difficult practice, but you must observe and examine yourself throughout the day. Are you just repeating yesterday on autopilot?

这很难，但你必须全天观察审视自己：你是不是在惯性重复昨天？

When you read an opinion or belief, are you going to solidify your own stance further by arguing with them in your head and saying how wrong they are?

看到不同观点时，你是否会在心里反驳，强化自己的立场？

When you sit with this uncomfortable feeling without making a decision, you train your nervous system to tolerate the gap between impulse and action.

当你带着不适感坐着，不做任何决定，就在训练神经系统忍受冲动与行动之间的空隙。

From that neutral state, it’s much easier to make a conscious choice in the right direction.

在这种中立状态下，更容易做出清醒的正向选择。

Now, there are plenty of ways to “train” his gap. They’re very popular right now. Meditation, cold exposure, fasting, etc. Anything that helps you delay gratification is useful.

现在有很多训练这个空隙的方法，很流行：冥想、冷水浴、断食等，任何帮助你延迟满足的方法都有用。

But in my opinion, the most impactful thing you can do is practice somewhat of a 24/7 meditation. You become the observer. You operate from a higher level.

但在我看来，最有影响力的是练习全天候冥想：成为观察者，以更高维度运作。

You attempt to get really good at expanding and contracting your mind like you would when you’re training a muscle. You zoom out to think clearly, and zoom in to act.

试着像练肌肉一样，熟练放大与收缩心智：拉远视角清晰思考，拉近视角果断行动。

The problem is that people live in a perpetually zoomed-in state. Survival mode. Notice it. Do nothing about it. Neutralize the impulse and choose your own direction.

问题是人们总活在 “拉近视角” 的生存模式里。觉察它，不做反应，中和冲动，选择自己的方向。

Winning the game is how you discover it’s the wrong game.

赢下这场游戏，你才会发现它本就是一场错的游戏

The greatest trait you can acquire is to work with tremendous intensity on things that matter to you, and more importantly, be strangely unbothered when things don’t work out.

你能拥有的最棒特质是：对重要的事全力以赴，更重要的是，事与愿违时能异常淡然。

If you’re smart, you’ve noticed something. We replaced one survival game with another. Meaning, we replaced one source of suffering with another.

聪明的话你会发现：我们只是把一种生存游戏换成另一种，把一种痛苦来源换成另一种。

Sure, learning how to stress yourself into building a hyper successful business or aesthetic physique is better (in my opinion) than stressing yourself into a hole of being overweight and broke, but the problem is this:

当然，逼着自己打造成功事业或好身材，在我看来比逼着自己肥胖贫穷要好，但问题在于：

The business owner will be unhappy until they hit a certain level of money, like $100m dollars, but even once they hit that number, they still have the same identity.

商人赚到 1 亿美元前都不开心，赚到后，自我还是没变。

If they don’t increase the amount and continue working, they will remain unhappy.

如果不继续赚钱、继续工作，还是会不开心。

The same happens for the bodybuilder. They won’t be happy until they win their pro card, but unless they have a shift in who they are, they will continue moving the goal post until they have an existential crisis and are forced to change.

健身者也是如此，拿到职业卡前不开心；除非自我转变，否则会不断提高目标，直到陷入存在危机，被迫改变。

This is the crucial piece that so many are missing. I’m sure you know of many business or fitness influencers who represent this archetype of drowning out their lack of fulfillment with more work.

这是很多人缺失的关键。我相信你见过很多商业或健身博主，用更多工作掩盖内心的空虚。

The goal, then, is to play the survival game, because there is a great deal of fulfillment that comes from continuous growth (and life is a bitch if you stagnate), but you must also transcend the game.

所以目标是：参与这场生存游戏（因为持续成长会带来巨大满足感，停滞的人生很糟糕），但同时超越这场游戏。

You remain largely unbothered when things don’t work out.

事与愿违时，你基本能淡然处之。

The question then is, how does one become unbothered? Well, you can’t try to achieve that, because the bothered self attempting to become the unbothered self is just another survival strategy.

问题是：如何做到淡然？你不能刻意追求，因为焦虑的自我试图变成淡然的自我，本身就是另一种生存策略。

The person who says “I am going to detach from outcomes” has just made not being attached to outcomes the outcome they’re attached to. So it becomes the axis of their suffering. The snake eats its tail.

说 “我要放下结果” 的人，恰恰把 “放下结果” 变成了执念，成为痛苦的核心，陷入恶性循环。

That’s a big trap here... premature transcendence. You read about detachment or spirituality and immediately identify with it because it sounds like a “higher” operating mode.

这是一个巨大陷阱：过早的超脱。你读到放下、灵性，就立刻认同，因为听起来像“更高阶” 的运作模式。

It’s a status game of who’s more enlightened. In reality, this is avoidance.

这是比谁更 “开悟” 的身份游戏，本质上是逃避。

I was hesitant to put this section in the letter, because most people have to spend years deeply attached before their attachment becomes visible enough to see through.

我犹豫过要不要写这部分，因为大多数人要执念多年，才能看清自己的执念。

I know content is supposed to provide something immediately actionable so you can get your cheap dopamine hit disguised as learning and success, but this is a decade-long project.

我知道内容本该提供即时可操作的方法，让你获得伪装成学习与成功的廉价多巴胺，但这是一项长达十年的工程。

What you can do, however, is this:

但你可以做这些：

First, continue increasing the gap between impulse and response. Work your psychic muscle. Expand and contract the mind, and be aware of it. Become very familiar with what those states of consciousness feel like.

第一，继续拉长冲动与回应的距离，锻炼心智肌肉，放大与收缩心智，并觉察它，熟悉这些意识状态的感受。

Second, distinguish pain from suffering. Pain is a feature of life. Suffering is the identity’s refusal to accept the event.

第二，区分痛苦与煎熬：痛苦是人生的常态，煎熬是自我拒绝接受事实。

Becoming more conscious and open-minded doesn’t remove pain, but it removes the second layer.

变得更清醒、更开放，不会消除痛苦，但会消除第二层煎熬。

The businessman whose revenue drops still feels the punch, but he doesn’t have to feel the subsequent spiral of “this shouldn’t be happening to me, I’m a failure, my life is ruined.”

营收下滑的商人仍会感到冲击，但不必陷入 “这不应该发生、我是失败者、人生毁了” 的恶性循环。

The intense and unbothered personality is powerful. It is near the pinnacle of human development across both material and spiritual planes.

全力以赴又淡然处之的人格极具力量，接近物质与精神层面的人类发展顶峰。

They are a walking contradiction. They fear mediocrity enough to work hard, and they’re unbothered enough to not collapse when things don’t work out.

他们是行走的矛盾体：因害怕平庸而极度努力，因足够淡然而在受挫时不崩溃。

It is the mark of a mature mind to be able to hold two opposing forces at once.

能同时容纳两种对立力量，是心智成熟的标志。

I hope that you are able to one day master that state. Until then, thank you for reading. —— Dan

希望你有一天能掌控这种状态。感谢阅读。——Dan Koe
